Method & limitations

What the numbers mean — and what they can’t tell you.

The detector

Scores come from GPTZero’s API, a model trained to classify documents as human, mixed, or AI. We show the three class probabilities it returns, its confidence category, and the detector version.

A class probability is the detector’s estimated likelihood that a label fits the whole document. GPTZero describes the probability for the predicted class as the chance its prediction is correct on similar documents. It is not the percentage of words written by AI.

We never compute a score ourselves. If the detector is unavailable, you’ll see “Score unavailable” — never a substitute number.

The writing tools

Humanize, Fix Grammar, Simplify, and Summarize use a general-purpose language model (OpenAI or Anthropic, depending on how this site is configured). Each mode has narrow instructions: preserve meaning, facts, names, numbers, and quotations; don’t add claims.

Language models still make mistakes. Always compare the revision with your original. Humanize aims for natural phrasing — it is not a way to evade detectors, and we make no promise about how any detector will score the result.

What no detector can do

  • Prove who wrote a text, or prove that someone used AI.
  • Reliably identify which AI tool or model was used.
  • Judge short passages, lists, code, poetry, or heavily edited text with confidence.
  • Avoid false positives — human writing, especially formal or non-native English writing, can be flagged.

Use a result as one signal for a conversation, never as the sole basis for academic, hiring, or disciplinary decisions.

FAQs

Straight answers.

What does the AI score mean?

You’ll see three numbers from GPTZero: the probability that the document as a whole is human-written, mixed (part human, part AI), or AI-generated. They add up to about 100%. The highest one is the detector’s prediction. A 70% “AI” probability does not mean 70% of the words came from AI.

Can this prove someone used ChatGPT or another AI?

No. Detectors estimate how closely text resembles patterns seen in AI-generated writing. They make mistakes in both directions and cannot identify the specific tool. Treat any result as a prompt for discussion, not evidence.

Will Humanize make my text pass AI detectors?

We don’t promise that, and you shouldn’t rely on it. Humanize asks a language model for natural phrasing with conservative edits that keep your meaning. You can check the revision with the detector yourself — that’s a separate, explicit action.

Does a revision replace my original?

No. Your original stays in the editor. Revisions appear in the results panel, where you can compare changes, copy, or download them. If you choose “Use as my text”, you can restore the original with one click.

Which files can I upload?

.txt and .md (up to 2 MB), and .pdf and .docx (up to 10 MB). Text is extracted in your browser, and you review it before using it. Password-protected files and scanned (image-only) PDFs can’t be read — paste the text instead. Up to 2,500 words or 20,000 characters can be processed at once.

Is my text stored?

This site doesn’t store or log your text. When you run a tool, the text passes through our server to the one provider that performs it — GPTZero for detection, or the configured language-model provider for writing tools. Their own policies then apply; see . Avoid submitting confidential or personal information.

Is it really free?

Yes, there’s no account or payment. The site owner pays the providers, so there are fair-use limits per minute and per day. If a limit is reached, you’ll see a clear message.
